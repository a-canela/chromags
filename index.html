01 — Overview

Recolor an animatable avatar with words.

Given a trained animatable 3D Gaussian head avatar and a free-form instruction such as “red matte lipstick” or “skin lighter and green eyes”, ChromaGS recolors exactly the requested semantic regions in about 50 ms, directly at render time: no retraining, no diffusion, and the same result every time.

Abstract

We present ChromaGS, a method for real-time, language-guided color editing of animatable 3D Gaussian head avatars. Given a trained animatable avatar, users can instantly modify the color of semantic regions through natural language, with edits applied at render time and no retraining required.

Our key insight is to augment each Gaussian primitive with learned soft assignments to semantic regions and decompose colors into region-level base colors and Gaussian-level residuals. This decomposition enables coherent color transfer: modifying a region’s base color propagates naturally through all associated Gaussians while preserving fine appearance details encoded in residuals. A two-stage language pipeline translates text instructions into target colors, supporting both absolute specifications and relative adjustments. Unlike generative editing methods that may introduce unintended modifications, our approach provides deterministic, precisely localized semantic control. Experiments demonstrate faithful appearance preservation and intuitive interaction across diverse subjects.

Contributions

  1. 1A semantic Gaussian representation in which every primitive carries learned soft assignments to facial regions (hair, eyebrows, eyes, lips, skin), enabling localized color editing that stays consistent across expressions and viewpoints.
  2. 2A color decomposition that separates region-level base colors from per-Gaussian residuals, so that changing a base color recolors the whole region while texture, shading and specular highlights are preserved.
  3. 3A two-stage language pipeline (semantic parsing + deterministic color resolving) for robust, intuitive color specification, handling both absolute (“olive skin”) and relative (“darker”) instructions.

Method

ChromaGS builds on a FLAME-bound animatable Gaussian avatar. Gaussians learn which region they belong to from SAM3 masks, and what color they show is split into a shared region base color plus a local residual. A text instruction is turned into new base colors, which are swapped in at render time.

(a) · Semantic masks

Open-vocabulary segmentation with SAM3

For each semantic class (hair, eyebrows, eyes, lips, skin) and each training frame, SAM3 is prompted with text to produce a binary mask Mk. Iris masks are obtained from the eye masks by detecting ellipses and warping an annular template.

Masks are made mutually exclusive by prioritizing smaller regions (e.g. iris over sclera) to ensure proper layering. SAM3 is temporally consistent, and since supervision is aggregated over hundreds of frames, occasional segmentation noise is naturally absorbed during training.

(b) · Text-guided color editing

Parse, then resolve

Semantic parsing. A fine-tuned Flan-T5-Small turns the instruction into a structured string region, color, type, with multiple edits separated by semicolons. This small output space is far easier to learn than direct RGB prediction.

“make skin darker and blue eyes”
→ skin, darker, relative; eyes, blue, absolute

Color resolving is deterministic:

  • Absolute (“olive skin”): the description is embedded with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 and matched by cosine similarity against region-specific palettes of anatomically plausible colors (e.g. Fitzpatrick skin tones, melanin-based iris colors).
  • Relative (“darker”, “more saturated”): the current region color is converted to HSV and shifted by deterministic modifiers.
(c) · Semantic-aware Gaussians

Semantic logits and color decomposition

Each Gaussian gets semantic logits si ∈ ℝK with soft probabilities pi = softmax(si), rendered with the differentiable rasterizer and supervised by a pixel-wise cross-entropy against per-class blurred masks. Its color is a semantic blend of region base colors plus an expression-dependent SH residual:

cifinal = Σk pi,k bk + cires

Editing simply swaps base colors, so only the targeted regions shift while residuals keep the details:

ciedited = cifinal + Σk pi,k (bktarget − bk)

Cross-region adaptation: classes are weighted mixes of mask channels (e.g. skin also draws on lips at 0.3 and eyebrows at 0.2), so a skin-tone edit shifts pigment-correlated regions coherently.

CLIP measures image–text alignment (averaged over ViT-L/14, ViT-B/16 and ViT-B/32), Identity is the ArcFace cosine similarity between edited and unedited renders, and Context is 1 − LPIPS outside the target region. ChromaGS achieves substantially higher identity and context preservation, reflecting its ability to confine edits precisely to the target region. The baselines’ slightly higher CLIP scores reflect a limitation of the metric itself: a diffusion editor that tints the entire face green when asked for “green eyes” produces a stronger global color signal that CLIP rewards, even though the edit is spatially incorrect.
